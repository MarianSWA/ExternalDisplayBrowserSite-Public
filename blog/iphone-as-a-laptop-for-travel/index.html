Using an iPhone as a Laptop for Travel: The No-Computer Setup That Actually Works

Leaving the laptop at home is a lovely idea until you need to do something that needs a real screen. This is the setup that closes that gap: a phone, a small bag of accessories, and a desktop that appears on whatever monitor or TV happens to be in the room. It weighs a few hundred grams and it fits in a jacket pocket.

What's in the bag

ItemWhy
USB-C multiport adapterHDMI for the screen, USB for a drive, pass-through power so the phone charges. The single most important piece.
Short HDMI cableHotel and rental TVs have a port; the cable is usually somewhere else.
Folding Bluetooth keyboardReal typing. Doesn't need to be expensive.
Small Bluetooth mouseTurns a big screen into a workstation. Optional, your phone's screen is a trackpad.
Bus-powered USB-C SSDPhotos, footage, backups. Mounts directly.
Charger with enough wattageDriving a display and a drive uses power faster than you'd think.

That's under half a kilo, and nothing in it needs its own charger except the phone.

The software half

XeOS: External Display Browser is what turns the screen into something usable. Stock iOS mirrors your phone: a phone-shaped picture in the middle of a widescreen panel, with black bars either side. XeOS instead renders a desktop at the display's own resolution, windows, a dock, a file manager, a desktop browser.

What a working day looks like

Email, docs and spreadsheets

Because the browser is a desktop browser on a desktop-sized screen, Gmail, Google Docs and Sheets serve their full interfaces rather than the mobile ones. Sheets gets a formula bar. Docs gets proper keyboard shortcuts. Install each as its own dock app and you skip the tab-hunting entirely.

Files, including the SSD

The Files app mounts folders from iCloud Drive, from the phone itself, and from a USB drive in the adapter. Once mounted, you open, edit and save in place, a card full of photos becomes browsable, an iCloud folder becomes a working directory.

PDFs and documents

A full PDF viewer with search, annotation and form filling, plus preview for Word, Excel and PowerPoint files people send you. Signing a contract in a hotel room stops being a problem.

Presenting

If the trip includes a talk, a PDF of the deck and the green full-screen button is the entire workflow, no network needed. The presenting guide covers this and the Google Slides route.

Evenings

Same setup, TV instead of a monitor: YouTube full screen with the built-in ad blocker on, or a slideshow of the day's photos straight from your library. Full-screen YouTube and slideshows each have their own guide.

Where the phone is better than a laptop

  • Weight and bulk. No comparison.
  • Connectivity. Your data plan is already on it, and no hotel Wi-Fi captive portal to fight.
  • Airport security. Nothing to take out of the bag.
  • Theft risk. A phone stays in your pocket; a laptop stays in a room.
  • Battery. With a pass-through adapter, you're charging while you work.

Where it isn't

Be realistic about this before you commit to a trip:

  • Your other iPhone apps don't run in the desktop. What runs is the browser, the built-in apps, and websites you install. If your work needs a specific native app on the big screen, this isn't it.
  • Heavy editing, video, audio, large design files, is not what this is for.
  • Some embedded page controls, consent banners, certain sign-in and payment frames, need one tap on the phone's screen. There's a button for it; see Device Layout Mode.

For writing, email, spreadsheets, research, documents, presenting, admin and media, which is most of what most people travel with a laptop to do, it holds up.

Set it up before you go

  1. Install XeOS and run through setup once at home with your own monitor.
  2. Import your bookmarks and passwords, logging in on the road with a trackpad is otherwise painful.
  3. Install the sites you use as dock apps.
  4. Mount your iCloud Drive working folder.
  5. Export anything you must be able to open offline to PDF.
  6. Pair the keyboard and mouse and check they reconnect.

Ten minutes at home saves an hour in a hotel room.

Frequently asked

Will the phone charge while driving a display?

With a multiport adapter that has pass-through power, yes. A plain USB-C to HDMI cable carries no power.

What if the room only has a TV with no free HDMI cable?

Carry a short one. It's the accessory people forget and the one that ruins the plan.

Can I work without a mouse?

Yes, the phone's screen acts as a trackpad, with long-press-then-drag for dragging.

Does it work on an iPad too?

Yes, including older iPads with no Stage Manager support, see the iPad article.
